# Teaching Visualizer

Use this skill to prepare a small tutoring lesson that helps students explain what causes each result. Keep one concept in focus and make the learner predict, act, check, and explain.

## When to use

- A tutor needs a visual explanation, lesson card, or small activity for junior-high electricity.
- A student can recite a rule but cannot explain the physical cause.
- A lesson should include a question and a simple experiment or paper/tablet simulation.

Do not turn this into a full chapter, decorative poster, or difficult problem set unless requested.

## Lesson recipe

1. State one core question and the misconception it addresses.
2. Give a compact visual plan: components, labels, arrows, colors, and a cause-and-effect chain. Prefer a simple text diagram when no image renderer is requested.
3. Ask two questions before revealing the answer. Include what evidence or reasoning to listen for.
4. Give one activity with **Predict → Operate → Observe → Explain** steps. Use common materials where practical; offer a paper/tablet simulation when equipment is unavailable.
5. Close with one accurate rule in the student's own causal language, ideally “because … therefore …”.

## Physics and safety checks

- Distinguish conventional current direction from electron drift when direction matters. Do not depict charge as being used up by a lamp; energy is transferred while charge circulates in a closed circuit.
- For series/parallel classification, trace branches and identify nodes. Two components connected across the same pair of nodes are in parallel even if a drawing is rearranged.
- For a rheostat, state which terminals are connected. The effective resistance depends on the wire length between the slider and the selected fixed terminal; “slider moves right” alone does not determine the result.
- Use only low-voltage cells and suitable classroom components. Never instruct learners to connect a cell directly across itself, bypass a lamp with a wire, or create a short circuit. Discuss short circuits with a diagram only.
- Treat analogies as limited models and name what they do not explain.
- Separate what the diagram predicts from what an experiment actually observes; brightness can depend on the lamp, cell, and connections.

## Output format

Use these headings unless the user requests another format:

1. **核心问题与误区**
2. **一页理解图**
3. **先猜一猜** (two questions)
4. **动手验证** (materials, prediction, action, observation, explanation, safety)
5. **规律总结**
6. **家教追问** (one or two prompts that reveal the student's reasoning)

Keep text brief enough to present on a tablet. Put the answer or explanation after each question so the tutor can hide it while the student predicts.

## Common mistakes

- Giving the definition before a question: start with a prediction instead.
- Asking only for an answer: ask the student to name the changed quantity and the causal chain.
- Assuming a component's behavior from its visual position: follow actual connections and nodes.
- Suggesting unsafe circuit construction to demonstrate a fault: use a paper diagram and explain the risk instead.
